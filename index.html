Scale definition: 1.5–25 (levels 1–6). Extreme, clinically implausible combinations can exceed 25 numerically; level stays 6.

How it works

Comprehensive = Basic score of the primary procedure (mortality + morbidity + difficulty, Table B2) + procedure-specific factors (Table B3, when published) + patient factors (Table B4).

Source: Lacour-Gayet F, Clarke D, Jacobs J, et al. The Aristotle score: a complexity-adjusted method to evaluate surgical results. European Journal of Cardio-Thoracic Surgery 2004;25(6):911–924. doi:10.1016/j.ejcts.2004.03.027.
Educational reference tool. Based on a 2004 expert-consensus scoring system whose validation was still underway at publication [see paper §6.2]. Not a substitute for clinical judgement or the official Aristotle Committee materials.
